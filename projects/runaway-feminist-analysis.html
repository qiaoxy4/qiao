Graduation Thesis · Literary Research

An Interpretation of Runaway from a Feminist Perspective

Examining how domestic space, family relationships, and broader social constraints shape women's agency through Alice Munro's short story Runaway.

Period

Oct. 2023 – Jun. 2024

Research Focus

Feminist Literary Criticism

Project Type

Undergraduate Thesis

01 / Context & Motivation

When leaving does not mean being free.

Alice Munro's Runaway follows Carla, a woman whose attempt to leave her domestic life becomes complicated by emotional attachment, social expectations, and her dependence on others.
Rather than treating her decision as a simple choice between staying and leaving, this research examines the conditions that shape her ability to act independently.

02 / Research Questions

What makes autonomy difficult to achieve?

QUESTION 01

How does domestic space shape Carla's choices and sense of independence?

QUESTION 02

How do family relationships and emotional dependencies influence her decisions?

QUESTION 03

What social and personal constraints contribute to her return?

03 / Analytical Framework

Looking beyond individual decisions.

The analysis applies a feminist perspective to examine the relationship between individual agency and the social conditions surrounding Carla. Three interconnected dimensions guide the reading.

01

Domestic Space

The home as a space shaped by domestic responsibilities, dependence, and unequal relationships.

02

Relational Dependence

The influence of family relationships, emotional attachment, and interpersonal expectations on individual choices.

03

Social Constraints

The broader expectations and structural inequalities that limit women's autonomy.

04 / Central Contradiction

Resistance and return

Initial Condition

Domestic Life

A life shaped by dependence and constrained choices.

→

Attempted Escape

→

Outcome

Return

The decision to return reveals the complexity of autonomy.

Carla's return complicates the idea that leaving an oppressive environment automatically leads to independence. Her choices must be understood in relation to emotional, domestic, and social constraints.

05 / Textual Analysis

Understanding the conditions behind her choices.

Domestic Dependence

The analysis examines how Carla's domestic circumstances and dependence on others influence her capacity to make independent decisions. The home is considered not merely as a physical setting, but as part of the relationships and expectations shaping everyday life.

Female Agency

Carla's attempt to escape reflects a desire for autonomy. However, the tension between resistance and return raises questions about how women's agency is exercised within unequal social and personal circumstances.

Failed Escape

The research considers the factors that complicate Carla's attempt to leave, including emotional attachment, the challenges of independent living, and broader social constraints.

Female Solidarity

The role of the neighboring woman is also considered, particularly how support from another woman creates an alternative perspective on Carla's situation and her possibilities for change.

06 / Key Insight

Autonomy is not simply about having the freedom to leave.

It also depends on the social, emotional, and material conditions that make independent choices possible.

07 / Reflection

From literary analysis to understanding everyday life.

This research strengthened my interest in looking beyond individual behavior to understand the relationships and environments that shape people's choices. It also influenced how I approach questions about everyday experiences: rather than assuming that people always have equal freedom to act, I became more attentive to the conditions that enable or constrain their agency.
