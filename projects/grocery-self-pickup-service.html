A DIFFERENT WAY TO SHOP

Convenience Beyond Speed

Rethinking the relationship between delivery speed, pickup schedules, and everyday shopping routines.

01 · PROJECT OVERVIEW

Can a slower service offer a more convenient experience?

On-demand delivery has made speed a defining feature of everyday shopping. Consumers can order groceries and receive them without leaving home or planning far ahead.

Yet convenience is not determined by speed alone. Delivery arrangements, collection schedules, purchasing habits, and the effort required to obtain an order all influence the experience.

This project explored grocery self-pickup as an alternative to on-demand delivery. Through a comparison of the two service models and interviews with consumers, I examined how different arrangements shape shopping decisions and perceptions of convenience.

The project also explored how a self-pickup service could offer more flexible collection arrangements through a simple visual prototype.

02 · CONTEXT & PROBLEM

When speed becomes the standard

On-demand delivery platforms emphasize fast fulfillment and immediate satisfaction. As these services become familiar, speed can gradually shape what consumers expect from everyday shopping.

Self-pickup offers a different arrangement: users place orders online and collect their purchases at a later time.

However, a fixed collection schedule can create another constraint. Users may need to reorganize their daily routines around the service, reducing the appeal of self-pickup.

05 · KEY FINDINGS

Convenience is not simply about speed

01

Fixed pickup schedules can become a constraint

The research identified fixed collection times as an inconvenience. Even when users choose self-pickup, they may need to adjust their daily schedules to meet the collection window.

02

Flexibility influences willingness to self-pick up

The flexibility of collection arrangements affects whether users are willing to choose self-pickup. This suggests that the timing of collection is an important part of the overall service experience.

03

Platforms shape expectations of convenience

The project also examined how platforms' emphasis on delivery speed and immediate satisfaction shapes users' expectations of convenience. Speed can become a familiar benchmark, even though users' everyday needs may involve more than receiving an order quickly.

06 · DESIGN EXPLORATION

Designing around everyday schedules

Based on the research exploration, I developed a visual interface prototype illustrating a grocery self-pickup experience.

The prototype covered three key stages: product selection, pickup time selection, and order confirmation. Multiple pickup time slots were designed to explore how users could have greater flexibility in arranging their collections.

DESIGN DIRECTION

More flexible collection arrangements

The prototype explored multiple pickup time slots as a way to give users more choice over when they collect their orders. The interface remained a visual design exploration and was not user-tested.

07 · REFLECTION

Convenience should adapt to everyday life, not only optimize service speed.

This project challenged my initial understanding of convenience as a matter of efficiency. Comparing delivery and self-pickup helped me consider how different service models organize people's time and everyday routines.

A service may reduce waiting time while introducing new constraints elsewhere. This made me consider a broader question in design: rather than asking how people can adapt to a more efficient system, how might services better accommodate the rhythms of everyday life?

PROJECT SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

This project involved comparative exploration, consumer interviews, and the development of a visual interface prototype. The proposed pickup time options were not tested with users, and the prototype should be understood as a design exploration rather than a deployed or validated service.
